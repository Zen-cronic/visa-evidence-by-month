# Evidence rules

Read before Step 1. These rules decide which months are planned, which photos are chosen, how each line is labelled and how gaps are named.

## Planning months (Step 3)

1. Start from the month counts returned by the timeline summary. Never plan a month with zero photos of both partners.
2. If the number of months with photos is at or below the photo limit, plan all of them.
3. Otherwise spread the plan evenly across the whole relationship: always the first and the latest month with photos, then the months that best fill the longest stretches between planned months.
4. Prefer months that also have no joint document, so photos cover what documents do not.

## Choosing photos (Step 4)

- Check only the sample photos returned for planned months.
- A photo is fit to send only if it passes every rule, in order: both partners visible; both faces visible and not covered; a real photo, not a screenshot or a photo of a screen or print; suitable to show a government officer (no nudity, intimate or sexual content); not blurry.
- Count every failed photo by the first rule it failed, and show the counts.
- Choose the first passing photo in each planned month. At most one photo per calendar day across the whole pack, so the pack is not filled with near-identical shots.
- Never pad. If fewer photos pass than the limit, keep the smaller number and say so.

## Labelling each line

The official checklist asks for "a brief description of context of each photo". These four fields are this Skill's way of helping the couple write it; they are not an official format.

| Field | Source | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Date | capture date in photo metadata | `from metadata` |
| Place | location in photo metadata | `from metadata`, or `unknown` |
| People | the two confirmed Photos people names | `from Photos people` |
| Context | the visual description | `[confirm]`; saved as `[not confirmed]` unless the user confirms or edits it |

A visual description can say "two people at a restaurant table" but cannot know it was an anniversary dinner. Only the couple can add that.

## Coverage and gaps (Step 6)

Three columns per month:

- **In your library:** the number of photos where face grouping found both partners.
- **In the pack:** a chosen photo from that month.
- **Joint document:** a document naming both partners, dated in that month or covering it.

A gap is a month with no photo of both partners found by face grouping and no joint document. Always label it that way. A gap can be a face-grouping miss (one partner turned away, a new haircut, photos in a library this account cannot open), so the index lists the libraries searched and suggests checking those months by hand. The Skill never states that the couple was apart.

For common-law couples, the table shows at a glance whether the latest 12 months are continuous. It states the facts only; it never says whether an officer will accept them.

## Joint documents (Step 5)

Useful kinds: a lease or mortgage naming both, utility or internet bills, insurance, bank statements, official mail to the same address. Keep only documents naming both partners. Note differences in spelling or address without changing anything; the couple may need to explain them.

Many people photograph papers with their phone instead of scanning them. Step 5b finds those photos and reads their text:

- A photo of a document counts as joint only when its recognised text contains both partners' names as written. Names, addresses and dates are copied from the text, never guessed or completed.
- Each one is labelled `from a photo of a document` in the index, with a note to upload a clean scan or the original if the officer needs it.
- If the same paper exists as a scan and a photo, the scan is used and the photo is noted.
- A photo of a document is never a relationship photo: it fails rule 3 in Step 4 and has no faces to group.
