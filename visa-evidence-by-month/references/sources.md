# Sources

The official wording this Skill's rules rest on, quoted exactly. Rules differ by country and change; the user checks their own current checklist and tells the Skill what it asks. Canada is the worked example (pages fetched on 2026-11-12); New Zealand, Australia, the UK and the US follow (pages fetched on 2026-11-15).

## Photos: IMM 5533, Document Checklist: Spouse

- **PDF:** https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/ircc/documents/pdf/english/kits/forms/imm5533/01-09-2022/imm5533e.pdf
- **Version:** "IMM 5533 (09-2022) E".
- **Landing page:** https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/application/application-forms-guides/imm5533.html ("Last updated: September 2022").

When photos are needed. The form asks four questions:
- "a) Are you and your spouse currently living together?"
- "b) Do you and your spouse have any children together?"
- "c) Is this a first marriage for both you and your sponsor?"
- "d) Have you and your sponsor been married for a minimum of two years as of the date of application?"

If you answer yes to all four: "If you checked this box, you do not need to provide any additional proof of relationship at this time." Otherwise:
- "e) If you did not answer yes to all four (4) of the above questions, also provide:"
- "Photos of your wedding, customary celebrations, engagement, and/or outings. Provide a maximum of 20 photographs to support your relationship (taken at different times and places)."
- "Please upload a separate document providing a brief description of context of each photo provided, label your photos accordingly"

The form asks only for a brief description of context. The Skill's date, place and people fields are its own way of helping you write that description. They are not an official requirement.

## Living together: IMM 5533, when the answer to a) is yes

"Pick at least two (2) of the following sets of documents."
- "Proof of joint ownership of residential property."
- "Rental agreement showing both you and your sponsor as occupants of a rental property."
- "Proof of joint utility accounts (eg. electricity, gas, telephone, internet), joint credit card accounts, or joint bank accounts. (Submit a minimum of 1 joint bill)."
- "Vehicle insurance showing that both you and your sponsor have been declared to the insurance company as residents of the insured's address."
- "Copies of government issued documents for you and your sponsor showing the same address (eg.: driver's licenses)"
- "Other documents issued to you or your sponsor showing the same address, whether the accounts are held jointly or not"

This list is from the spouse checklist. Common-law partners use IMM 5589 (https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/ircc/documents/pdf/english/kits/forms/imm5589/01-09-2022/imm5589e.pdf), whose text could not be read for this file.

## Not read, to check

- **IMM 5589, Document Checklist: Common-law Partner.** Not read for this file; check what it accepts as proof of living together.
- **IMM 5409, Statutory Declaration of Common-law Union.** Not read for this file; check whether your application needs it.

## Common-law partner

- **"Who you can sponsor"** (https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/family-sponsorship/spouse-partner-children/who-you-can-sponsor.html): "has lived with you for at least 12 consecutive months, meaning" / "you've lived together continuously for 1 year in a conjugal relationship, without any long periods apart" / "any time spent away from each other during the 12-month period should have been short and temporary".
- **Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations s. 1(1)** (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2002-227/page-1.html): "common-law partner means, in relation to a person, an individual who is cohabiting with the person in a conjugal relationship, having so cohabited for a period of at least one year."
- **Guide 5289** (https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/application/application-forms-guides/guide-5289-sponsor-your-spouse-common-law-partner-conjugal-partner-dependent-child-complete-guide.html): "To show that you are in a common-law relationship, submit proof that you:" / "share the same home," / "support each other financially and emotionally," / "have children together, if applicable," / "present yourselves in public as a couple."

## How the Skill uses these

| Skill rule | Source line |
|---|---|
| Photo limit defaults to 20 | "Provide a maximum of 20 photographs" |
| Photos spread across months and places | "taken at different times and places" |
| A line for each photo, confirmed by the couple | "a brief description of context of each photo provided, label your photos accordingly" |
| Joint documents naming both partners | IMM 5533 cohabitation list ("at least two (2)") |
| Married couples and common-law partners: joint papers, and papers in each partner's name showing the same address | IMM 5533: "whether the accounts are held jointly or not"; the common-law checklist (IMM 5589) was not read |
| Month-by-month coverage (a planning aid) | 12 consecutive months for common-law partners; for spouses there is no monthly requirement, only photos "taken at different times and places" |

## Australia: Partner visa (subclasses 820/801 and 309/100)

- **Page:** https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/partner-onshore/temporary-820 (Home Affairs; no "last updated" date shown). Fetched on 2026-11-15. The 309 page (https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/partner-offshore/provisional-309) lists the same four kinds of supporting evidence.

- De facto partners: "Usually you must be in a de facto relationship for at least 12 months immediately before you apply for this visa." / "show us you have been in your de facto relationship for at least 12 months before you applied for this visa."
- "Relationship history" / "Tell us in writing about:" / "how, when and where you first met" / "significant events in the relationship".
- Finances: "Evidence you and your partner share financial matters." / "joint mortgage or lease documents" / "joint bank account statements" / "household bills in both names."
- Your household: "household bills in both names" / "mail or emails addressed to you both" / "documents that prove your living arrangements."
- Social matters: "Evidence that others know about your relationship." / "You must provide statements from 2 witnesses" / "proof you do joint sporting, cultural or social activities together" / "proof you travel together."
- Commitment: "Evidence you are committed to a long-term relationship with each other."

The page's evidence list does not name photos of the couple.

**Not read, to check:** the 801 and 100 (permanent) pages; Form 888 itself.

**How the Skill uses these:** joint papers naming both cover "in both names" and "addressed to you both"; same-address papers are a planning aid for "documents that prove your living arrangements"; the month table can help plan the de facto "at least 12 months", without saying whether the relationship counts as de facto; photos only if the user's checklist asks for them.

## New Zealand: Partner of a New Zealander visas

- **Pages:** https://www.immigration.govt.nz/process-to-apply/once-you-have-a-visa/bringing-family-to-new-zealand/partnership-and-how-to-prove-it/ and https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visas/partner-of-a-new-zealander-resident-visa/ (Immigration New Zealand; no "last updated" date shown). Fetched on 2026-11-15.

- Resident Visa: "be living with your partner in a genuine and stable relationship for at least 12 months after you both turned 18 when you apply"
- "Living together means sharing the same home as your partner."
- "Evidence showing you are living together can include:" / "joint ownership of, or home loan for, residential property" / "a joint rental agreement or rental receipts" / "joint utility accounts, like power or phone bills" / "joint or individual mail sent to your shared address."
- "evidence of living together must cover a period of 12 months prior to the application being submitted."
- "You can provide us with items showing your shared address, dated every few months, to cover the full duration."
- "These items can be addressed to you both jointly — or individually — as long as we can see that you have both been at the same address for the same period of time."
- Genuine and stable relationship: "joint bank accounts being used frequently" / "social media posts or photos of you together" / "letters of support that recognise your partnership."
- Resident Visa: "you spend time together, for example, photos together, and emails and social media conversations between you"

The pages set no number of photos.

**Not read, to check:** the Partnership timeline and evidence checklist (PDF 11975); the Partner of a New Zealander Work Visa page.

**How the Skill uses these:** joint papers and same-address papers match "addressed to you both jointly — or individually"; the month table can help plan 12 months of "items showing your shared address, dated every few months", without saying whether the rule is met; photos of the two of you by month match "photos of you together", and the Skill's default of 20 photos is not from these pages.

## United Kingdom: Family visa as a partner or spouse

- **Page:** https://www.gov.uk/uk-family-visa/partner-spouse (GOV.UK; no "last updated" date shown on the page). Fetched on 2026-11-15.

- "you’ve been living together in a relationship for at least 2 years when you apply"
- "How to prove your relationship" / "Send evidence that:" / "comes from the government, a bank, landlord, utility provider, or a medical professional" / "confirms your relationship with your partner - for example, that you live together, share expenses or are married or in a civil partnership" / "is less than 4 years old"
- "a tenancy agreement, utility bills or Council Tax bills confirming that you live at the same address or pay bills together"
- "a bank statement from a joint bank account, or confirming that you live at the same address"
- "a letter from your doctor or dentist confirming that you live at the same address"
- If you do not live together: "communicate regularly with each other" / "spend time together as a couple, for example on holiday or at events"

The page does not name photos.

**Not read, to check:** Appendix FM-SE and Appendix FM of the Immigration Rules, for the exact evidence and definitions behind this guidance.

**How the Skill uses these:** joint papers and same-address papers match "confirming that you live at the same address"; the month table can help plan "at least 2 years" of living together, without saying whether it is met; the Skill does not check the "less than 4 years old" limit, so check each paper's date yourself; photos only if the user's checklist asks for them.

## United States: I-130 for a spouse, and the K-1 fiancé(e) visa

- **I-130 instructions:** https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/forms/i-130instr.pdf; form page https://www.uscis.gov/i-130 ("Edition Date 04/01/24", "Last Reviewed/Updated: 11/10/2026"). Fetched on 2026-11-15.
- **K-1 page:** https://www.uscis.gov/family/family-of-us-citizens/visas-for-fiancees-of-us-citizens ("Last Reviewed/Updated: 03/23/2018"). Fetched on 2026-11-15.

I-130, for a spouse: "you should submit one or more of the following types of documentation that may prove you have a bona fide marriage:"
- "(1) Documentation showing joint ownership of property;"
- "(2) A lease showing joint tenancy of a common residence, meaning you both live at the same address together;"
- "(3) Documentation showing that you and your spouse have combined your financial resources;"
- "(4) Birth certificates of children born to you and your spouse together;"
- "(5) Affidavits sworn to or affirmed by third parties having personal knowledge of the bona fides of the marital relationship."
- "(6) Any other relevant documentation to establish that there is an ongoing marital union."

The only photos the I-130 instructions ask for are "two identical color passport-style photographs of yourself and your spouse"; the Skill does not make these.

K-1: "You and your fiancé(e) met each other in person at least once within the 2-year period before you file your petition."

**Not read, to check:** the Form I-129F instructions (what proof of the in-person meeting they accept); the travel.state.gov K-1 page.

**How the Skill uses these:** joint papers naming both cover items (1) to (3); birth certificates are the linking papers for children, as in item (4); photos of the two of you by month can help find when you met in person, without saying whether it meets the K-1 rule; photos only if the user's checklist asks for them.
